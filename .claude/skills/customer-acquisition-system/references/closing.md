# Closing Toolkit (Bottom of Funnel)

Once outreach and follow-up produce a live conversation, closing is a repeatable process, not a
personality trait. The frame: **diagnose before you prescribe.** Weak closers pitch; strong
closers ask, qualify, and let the prospect talk themselves into the problem. Actual word-for-word
scripts live in `assets/scripts-library.md`; this file is the method.

## 1 — Discovery (earn the right to pitch)

Ask before you tell. The goal is to surface the problem, its cost, and the decision path — which
maps directly to **A-I-M** (Anxiety, Influence, Motivation; see `relational-acquisition.md`).
Cover, roughly in order:

- **Situation:** what they do now, current solution/vendor, how it's working.
- **Problem / Anxiety:** what's broken, what it risks. Dig past the first answer.
- **Impact:** quantify the cost of the problem — money, time, risk. This is what justifies price.
- **Influence / decision process:** who else is involved, what a "yes" requires internally.
- **Motivation / desired outcome:** what winning looks like and why now.
- **Timeline & budget reality:** when they need it solved and whether resources exist.

Talk less than half the time. The best discovery feels like a helpful expert, not an interrogation.

## 2 — Qualify (and disqualify fast)

A consistent rubric stops reps from wasting cadence on deals that won't close and from
disqualifying good ones on a hunch. Use a lightweight **Fit × Intent** score, or a named
framework if the team knows one (BANT = Budget/Authority/Need/Timeline for simple sales; MEDDIC
for complex B2B). Default rubric — score each 0–2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Need/pain** | none/vague | real but minor | urgent, quantified |
| **Fit (ICP)** | outside ICP | partial | ideal |
| **Authority** | no access to decider | influencer | decision-maker engaged |
| **Timeline** | "someday" | this year | this quarter |
| **Budget/means** | none | unclear | funded |

High scores → fast-track to proposal. Low scores → nurture track (don't discard) or disqualify.
**Disqualifying fast is a feature:** it protects rep time and keeps forecast honest. Record a
normalized `lost_reason` so patterns show up in the monthly review.

## 3 — Present / pitch (tied to what you heard)

Only now do you present — and only the parts that map to *their* discovered pains. Structure:
problem you heard → the specific outcome → how it works (briefly) → proof (case/number/name) →
price framed against the impact you quantified in discovery. Never a generic feature dump.

## 4 — Objection handling (a method, not a script war)

Objections are buying signals or missing information, rarely a flat "no." Use a consistent motion:
1. **Acknowledge** — don't argue or flinch ("Totally fair.").
2. **Clarify / isolate** — find the real objection ("Is it the price itself, or the timing?").
3. **Respond** — with proof, reframe, or a risk-reversal aimed at the *real* concern.
4. **Confirm & advance** — check it's resolved, then move to the next step.

The recurring objections — **price, timing, "need to think", partner/committee, incumbent,
trust/skepticism** — should each have a rehearsed response in `assets/scripts-library.md`. Build
the library from your own lost-deal reasons so it reflects reality, not generic sales-book lines.

## 5 — Close (ask clearly, remove friction)

- **Make the next step explicit and easy:** a specific time, a simple contract, a clear price.
  Ambiguity kills more deals than objections.
- **Use a low-pressure assumptive/summary close:** recap the value they agreed to, then ask
  directly ("Want to get started?" / "Shall we lock in Thursday?"). Then **stop talking.**
- **Trial-close along the way** ("Does that solve the empty-miles problem you mentioned?") so the
  final ask isn't a surprise.
- **If not now:** get a concrete reason and a next date, and route to the long-nurture track —
  never let a "not yet" fall out of the pipeline (see `follow-up.md`).

## 6 — Hand off to fulfillment (protect the customer capital)

A close is the *start* of the customer relationship, not the finish line. Every won deal triggers
a clean handoff to delivery: what was promised, by when, who owns it, and the first-value
milestone. This is where the system's **capacity operating rule** bites — do not keep closing
volume the fulfillment side can't absorb, or you convert wins into churn and reputational debt
(see `ai-governance.md` and the operating rule in `SKILL.md`). Set onboarding and the first
success moment deliberately; that's what makes LTV real and referrals possible.

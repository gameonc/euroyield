# Know Your Clientele — the ICP & Customer Avatar (Step Zero)

Before a single ad, cold call, or landing page, answer one question with precision: **who exactly
are we selling to, where are they, and where do they hang out?** Everything downstream depends on
it — your channels (where they *are*), your copy (how they *speak*), your offer (what they *want*),
and your targeting (who to even approach). A vague "anyone who needs X" is why most acquisition
money is wasted. **A sharp avatar beats a big audience every time.**

This is the foundation the profiles, cold outreach, copywriting, and Lead Engine all build on.

## The 10 dimensions of the avatar

Define these for the **one primary customer** first (you can add 2–3 more later — but never "everyone"):

1. **Who they are** — the identity.
   - *B2C:* age, gender, income, life stage, family, job, education.
   - *B2B:* industry, company size, revenue, and the **role/title** of the actual decision-maker
     (owner? ops manager? CFO?) — plus who influences them.
2. **Where they are** — geography and context. Local service area? A region? National? What's their
   situation right now that matters.
3. **Where they "speak" / hang out** — the platforms, communities, and media they *actually* use.
   This decides your channels. Which social (FB, IG, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube)? Which forums/groups
   (Reddit, Facebook groups, Nextdoor, industry Slacks)? What do they Google? What do they read,
   watch, listen to? What events/associations? **You go where they already are.**
4. **Psychographics** — values, identity, beliefs, fears, aspirations. Who do they want to *be*.
5. **Pains & problems** — what keeps them up at night; the problem you remove. (This is the
   *Anxiety* in A-I-M and the hook in your copy.)
6. **Desires & outcomes** — the result they actually want, in *their* terms. (The *Motivation*.)
7. **Objections & beliefs** — what stops them buying, and what they must believe to say yes.
8. **Buying triggers** — the events that make them ready *now* (a breakdown, a renewal, a move, a
   new hire, a regulation, a season). Timing beats persuasion.
9. **Their language (voice-of-customer)** — the exact words *they* use for the problem and the
   result. Copy that mirrors their words converts; marketer-speak doesn't.
10. **Awareness stage** — unaware → problem-aware → solution-aware → product-aware → most-aware.
    Cold traffic is usually problem/solution-aware; match the message to the stage
    (see `copywriting.md`).

## How to actually research it (don't guess — mine it)

- **Reviews** — yours *and competitors'* (Google, Yelp, Amazon, industry sites). Gold for pains,
  desires, and exact language.
- **Talk to real customers** — 5–10 conversations reveal more than any survey. Ask why they bought,
  what almost stopped them, what they'd tell a friend.
- **Communities** — read the forums/groups/subreddits where they complain and compare.
- **Search & keywords** — what they type tells you intent and language.
- **Sales-call notes & lost-reasons** — your own CRM (`measurement.md`) is a research goldmine.
- **Competitors** — who they target, how they position, where they advertise.

## The output: a one-page avatar (fill per business)

```
AVATAR — [business_id] — "[nickname, e.g. 'Steady-Lane Shipper Sam']"
WHO:        [demographics / firmographics + decision-maker role]
WHERE:      [geography / situation]
HANGS OUT:  [the 2–4 platforms/communities/media they actually use]  → your channels
PAINS:      [top 3 problems, in their words]
DESIRES:    [top 3 outcomes they want]
TRIGGERS:   [events that make them ready to buy now]
OBJECTIONS: [top 3 reasons they hesitate + the belief that overcomes each]
THEIR WORDS:[5–10 real phrases they use — feeds the copy]
AWARENESS:  [where cold traffic sits on the scale]
```

## How it wires into the rest of the system

- **Channels** ← dimension 3 ("where they speak"): pick channels where the avatar already is, not
  where it's easy (`cold-outreach.md`, and the profile's channel mix).
- **Copy & offer** ← dimensions 5–9: pains/desires/words/objections drive the offer and every ad,
  page, and email (`copywriting.md`).
- **Targeting/lists** ← dimensions 1–2: who and where to prospect (`cold-outreach.md` Step 1).
- **The profile** ← this fills the ICP/targeting and objection sections of `profiles/<business>.md`.
- **Close the loop:** the Lead Engine's captured data (which sources/geos/creatives actually convert,
  which cohorts have the best LTV) *refines* the avatar over time — the avatar is a hypothesis you
  sharpen with real data, not a one-time guess.

## The rule

**One primary avatar, defined sharply, beats a broad audience defined vaguely.** If you can't say
who your customer is, where they are, and where they hang out in a few sentences, you're not ready
to spend on acquisition — that's the first "Definition of Ready" check (`troubleshooting.md`).
